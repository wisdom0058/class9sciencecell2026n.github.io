6. How Cells Grow and Divide — Mitosis and Meiosis

Why bodies grow · onion root tip · cell cycle · mitosis (2 identical cells) · meiosis (4 gametes, half chromosomes) · cell culture · errors in division

2.4 How do normal cells grow and divide?

A small cut heals in a few days. Hair that falls out grows back. This happens because cells can grow and divide to replace old, dead or damaged cells. When your body grows, it is not mainly because cells get bigger — a cell can grow only up to a certain size. Growth happens because cells divide to form new cells, increasing their number.

Why can't a cell simply keep growing bigger? As a cell grows, its volume increases faster than its surface area. Everything the cell needs (oxygen, food) must enter through the surface, and wastes must leave through it. A very large cell would have too little surface to supply its large interior. Dividing keeps cells small and efficient.

Activity 2.5 — watching cells divide in an onion root tip

Place an onion bulb on a jar of water so that its base is immersed. In 5–6 days fresh roots grow.
Cut 2–3 cm of root tips and fix them in aceto-alcohol (glacial acetic acid : ethanol = 1 : 3) for 24 hours; then keep in 70% ethanol to preserve.
Put a root tip on a slide with a drop of dilute HCl for 10–15 minutes to soften the tissue; rinse.
Add 2–3 drops of aceto-carmine stain, wait 5–10 minutes, warm gently over a spirit lamp.
Cut off the very tip, add a cover slip and squash gently with your thumb to spread the cells in one layer. Observe.

Observation: the cells are not all alike. Some show tangled chromatin, some show distinct chromosomes lined up or being pulled apart, some show two new nuclei. This is because the cells at a growing root tip divide continuously, and different cells are caught at different stages of cell division (Fig. 2.17).

Every day an estimated hundreds of billions of cells in your body are replaced — almost 1% of all your cells. Both prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells divide, but eukaryotic cells do so in a more controlled, orderly sequence called the cell cycle.

2.4.1 Cell division — two major types

Cell division is the process by which new cells are formed from pre-existing cells. It allows organisms to grow, repair damaged tissues and reproduce. Skin cells, for instance, divide continuously to replace cells lost every day.

Mitosis (Fig. 2.18)

Every human begins life as a single fertilised egg. That one cell divides again and again to produce the trillions of cells in the body. Cells increase in number by mitosis, the most common type of division.

  • One parent cell → two daughter cells.
  • The daughter cells are genetically identical to each other and to the parent — the DNA is copied first, then shared equally, so each new cell gets the same DNA and the same number of chromosomes.
  • Purpose: growth, repair, maintenance and asexual reproduction. Genetic information is thus maintained across body cells.

Meiosis (Fig. 2.19)

Meiosis produces gametes and occurs only in the cells of reproductive organs — in animals, the testes (sperm) and ovaries (eggs); in plants, the anthers (pollen grains, which later produce sperm cells) and the ovaries (egg cells).

First division: the parent cell divides into two, and the chromosome number in each daughter cell is reduced to half.
Second division: similar to mitosis — each of the two cells divides again.
Result: four daughter cells (gametes), each with half the number of chromosomes (half the DNA) of the parent.
Fertilisation: when gametes from two individuals combine, the original chromosome number is restored — half from each parent.

Because meiosis shuffles and halves the chromosomes, the gametes are all different; children therefore resemble their parents but are not identical to them. This creates variation and diversity.

What if skin cells divided by meiosis instead of mitosis? (Pause and Ponder 7) The new cells would have only half the chromosomes and would not be identical to the surrounding skin cells; they could not function as proper skin cells, and the cut would not heal normally.
What if gametes were made by mitosis? They would carry the full chromosome number. At fertilisation the zygote would have double the normal number, and it would double again every generation — soon cells could not function and the species would not survive. There would also be no variation.

Cell culture — growing cells outside the body

Scientists can grow plant and animal cells outside the organism — this is cell culture. Cells are placed in a nutrient-rich medium and kept at the right temperature, correct acidity/alkalinity and moisture under sterile (germ-free) conditions, where they grow and multiply. Cell culture is crucial for studying how cells work and for producing biochemicals, food, medicines and vaccines.

Bridging science and society — when division goes wrong

  • Errors in mitosis → uncontrolled cell division, formation of tumours, and abnormal numbers of chromosomes in body cells.
  • Errors in meiosis → gametes with wrong chromosome numbers → genetic disorders (which may involve developmental problems or distinctive physical features), early pregnancy loss or reduced fertility.
Meet a scientist. Arun Kumar Sharma, a famous Indian botanist, is known for his work on chromosomes, plant taxonomy, evolution and development. He invented many laboratory methods for studying plant chromosomes and received the Shanti Swarup Bhatnagar Award and the Padma Bhushan.

Q1. Our body grows mainly because:
A. cells become very large
B. cells divide to form new cells
C. we eat more
D. cells stop dying
✔ Correct answer: B. cells divide to form new cells
A cell can grow only to a certain size; growth is by increase in cell number through division.
Q2. Mitosis produces:
A. four cells with half the chromosomes
B. two genetically identical cells with the same chromosome number
C. one large cell
D. gametes only
✔ Correct answer: B. two genetically identical cells with the same chromosome number
One division → 2 identical daughter cells; used for growth, repair and asexual reproduction.
Q3. Meiosis occurs in:
A. skin cells
B. all body cells
C. cells of reproductive organs only
D. bacteria
✔ Correct answer: C. cells of reproductive organs only
Only in testes/ovaries (animals) and anthers/ovaries (plants), to make gametes.
Q4. In meiosis, the parent cell divides:
A. once
B. twice, one after the other
C. three times
D. not at all
✔ Correct answer: B. twice, one after the other
Two successive divisions produce four gametes with half the chromosome number.
Q5. If a parent cell has 46 chromosomes, each gamete formed by meiosis has:
A. 46
B. 92
C. 23
D. 12
✔ Correct answer: C. 23
Meiosis halves the number: 46 → 23. Fertilisation restores 23 + 23 = 46.
Q6. Onion root tips are used to study cell division because:
A. they are green
B. their cells divide continuously
C. they have no nucleus
D. they are large
✔ Correct answer: B. their cells divide continuously
A growing root tip is a region of active, continuous cell division, so many stages can be seen.
Q7. Errors in meiosis may cause:
A. faster healing
B. genetic disorders or reduced fertility
C. more chlorophyll
D. larger cells
✔ Correct answer: B. genetic disorders or reduced fertility
Gametes with wrong chromosome numbers lead to genetic disorders, pregnancy loss or reduced fertility.
Q8. Growing cells in a nutrient medium outside the body under sterile conditions is called:
A. cell division
B. cell culture
C. mitosis
D. osmosis
✔ Correct answer: B. cell culture
Cell culture is used for research and to make medicines, vaccines and biochemicals.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Why do onion root tip cells look different from one another under the microscope?

Because the root tip is growing and its cells divide continuously. At any moment different cells are at different stages of division — some with tangled chromatin, some with visible chromosomes, some splitting in two.

What is the main difference between mitosis and meiosis?

Mitosis is one division giving two identical cells with the same chromosome number, used for growth and repair. Meiosis is two divisions giving four different gametes with half the chromosome number, used for sexual reproduction and variation.

Why must gametes have half the number of chromosomes?

So that when a sperm and an egg join at fertilisation, the zygote gets the normal full number — half from each parent. If gametes had the full number, the count would double every generation and cells could not function.

Why does a cut heal?

Skin cells near the cut divide by mitosis to produce new identical cells that fill the gap and replace the damaged ones.

Why is meiosis important for evolution?

It halves and shuffles chromosomes, so every gamete is different. Offspring therefore vary, and variation is the raw material on which natural selection works, letting species adapt to changing environments.
